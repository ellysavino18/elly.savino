Impact of Noise Pollution
on Stenella coeruleoalba

A spatial analysis assessing the conflict between acoustic disturbance and cetacean distribution in the Sicily Channel.

  1. Home
  2. Project Details

Context & Objectives

Context: The Sicily Channel is a highly productive Mediterranean biodiversity hotspot. Unfortunately, in this area the marine ecosystem and biodiversity is extremely affected by a great number of anthropogenic activities [1]. The striped dolphin (Stenella coeruleoalba) is listed as "Vulnerable" by the IUCN [2] and is increasingly threatened by anthropogenic noise [3] resulting from the expansion of industrial activities in the marine environment [4].

Objective: This project assesses the spatial impact of noise pollution on the distribution of striped dolphins in Sicily. It examines a set of human activities using noise sources identified as being of primary concern for cetacean conservation [5], assessing how intense levels of sound exposure can lead to habitat displacement [8].

Methodology

Spatial Data Gathering

Collected occurrence records for Stenella coeruleoalba from 2010 to 2023 using open-source datasets from GBIF and OBIS.

Mapping Human Activities

Mapped the primary sources of noise pollution utilising EMODnet data. Variables included marine traffic, offshore constructions, and coastal infrastructures like urban waste discharge points and aquaculture [6], [7].

GIS Analysis

Used QGIS (version 3.28.11) to overlay these variables, creating maps to visually analyse the spatial overlap between human activities and dolphin habitats.

Key Takeaways

  • Avoidance of High-Noise Areas

    The maps indicate a spatial conflict between striped dolphins and human activities. While occurrences are spread around the island, dolphins largely avoid the southern coast. This absence aligns with areas of high noise disturbance caused by offshore constructions and heavy vessel traffic.

  • Ineffectiveness of Current MPAs

    The spatial data shows that striped dolphins are not concentrated within the existing Marine Protected Areas (Natura 2000). Because these designated zones often coincide with major noise pollution drivers, they do not function as effective refuges for the species.

  • Conservation Implications

    The findings suggest that future MPAs require a more practical approach for realistic protection. Conservation efforts should prioritise submarine canyons and seamounts that are geographically separated from intense noise pollution and mining operations.
