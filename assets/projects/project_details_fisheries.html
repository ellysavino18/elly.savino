Age, Growth, and Feeding Ecology of Halobatrachus didactylus

A comprehensive fisheries assessment of the Lusitanian toadfish in Ria Formosa, Portugal.

  1. Home
  2. Project Details

Context & Objectives

Context: The Ria Formosa lagoon is a highly productive coastal ecosystem in southern Portugal, acting as a crucial nursery ground for numerous marine species. Understanding the population dynamics of local benthic predators like the Lusitanian toadfish (H. didactylus) is essential for local ecosystem management.

The Goal: Our goal was to conduct a full-cycle fisheries research project. Our international team was tasked with processing biological samples to estimate the age and model growth parameters, analsze reproductive dynamics, and assess the feeding ecology of the local toadfish population.

Methodology

Biological Sampling & Dissection

Conducted laboratory dissections to extract morphometric data, determine sex and maturity stages, and isolate stomachs and otoliths (sagittae) for further analysis.

Age & Growth Estimation

Extracted and processed otoliths, using both whole-reading and sectioning techniques to accurately estimate fish age.

Stomach Content Analysis (SCA)

Dissected stomachs and taxonomically identified prey items to calculate the Index of Relative Importance (%IRI), Frequency of Occurrence (%FO), and Vacuity Index (%VI).

Ecological Modelling

Utilised R Studio (version 2023.12.1+402) to fit the Von Bertalanffy growth model and analyse sex-specific growth patterns and life history strategies.

Key Takeaways

  • Evaluating Ageing Techniques:

    We actively compared methodologies for age determination, assessing the accuracy of reading sectioned otoliths versus whole otoliths to construct reliable age-length keys.

    • Whole sagittal and burnt sectioned otoliths of H. didactylus

  • Quantitative Dietary Profiling:

    Through Stomach Content Analysis (SCA) and the calculation of Index of Relative Importance (%IRI), we identified crustaceans, particularly brachyuran crabs, as a primary prey group, with secondary contributions from molluscs and fish.

  • Sex-Specific Growth Dynamics:

    By modelling the Von Bertalanffy growth parameters (L∞​, K, t0​) we identified distinct sexual dimorphism in growth rates, showing that males exhibit faster initial growth while females reach larger asymptotic lengths.

  • Reproductive Dynamics:

    By calculating Gonadosomatic (GSI) and Hepatosomatic (HSI) indices, we uncovered distinct, sex-specific energy allocation strategies. The data revealed a clear physiological trade-off: females utilise significant liver reserves (HSI) to support high gonadal investment (GSI) during the pre-spawning period, while males exhibit much lower reproductive energy costs.
