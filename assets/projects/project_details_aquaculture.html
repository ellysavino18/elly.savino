Octopus Under Threat:
A Co-Managed Approach to Reduce Illegal, Unreported and Unregulated Fishing (IUU) in the Algarve, Portugal

An academic research proposal designed to investigate IUU fishing through stakeholder engagement and co-management strategies.

  1. Home
  2. Project Details

Context & Objectives

Context: The common octopus (Octopus vulgaris) small-scale fishery is among the most profitable in Portugal, with the Algarve region accounting for 52% of national landings [1]. Based on an extensive literature review, we identified that despite employing over 700 people and involving 358 small-scale vessels, the fishery suffers from low compliance with minimum landing weight regulations (750g) and weak enforcement [2]. Currently, it is estimated that a significant portion of octopus landings in Portugal go unreported [3].

Objectives: As a theoretical academic exercise, our team developed a comprehensive qualitative research proposal to fill critical gaps in understanding Illegal, Unreported, and Unregulated (IUU) fishing [4]. The goal of the proposed study is to investigate the underlying drivers of IUU fishing, trace supply chain loopholes, and develop solutions in collaboration with the regional co-management committee (ParticiPESCA) [5].

Proposed Methodology

Literature Review

Reviewing peer-reviewed and grey literature following PRISMA guidelines to establish a foundation on co-management case studies and IUU policies [8].

Stakeholder Interviews

The design of anonymous, semi-structured qualitative interviews targeting 25-30 members of the fishing community, planning the use of snowball sampling [9] and MAXQDA for content analysis [10].

Participatory Workshops

A framework for facilitating focus group discussions [11] and backcasting exercises [12] with diverse stakeholders to co-develop interventions and a shared vision of a fishery without IUU practices.

Projected Outcomes & Implications

  • Quantifying the "Invisible" Catch

    By building trust through anonymous interviews, the research aims to transition IUU fishing from a general assumption into quantifiable data necessary for accurate stock assessments [1], [4].

  • Mapping the Supply Chain

    Investigating exactly where IUU octopus is sold, whether through direct recreational sales or auction loopholes—to identify ways to prevent illegal transactions [2].

  • Empowering Local Governance

    Delivering a final report and policy brief to the co-management committee, bridging the communication gap between authorities and fishers to foster compliance [13].
