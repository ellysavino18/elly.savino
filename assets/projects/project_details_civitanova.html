Water Current Impact
on the Civitanova Coast

A full-cycle oceanographic assessment of the Chienti River plume on the Civitanova coast, examining both benthic and pelagic ecosystems.

  1. Home
  2. Project Details

Context & Objectives

Context: Rivers are fundamental for human civilization, but continuous anthropogenic pressure has led to a deterioration of water quality. River runoff introduces inorganic nutrients (causing eutrophication) and contaminants (heavy metals, plastics) that threaten the achievement of the Good Environmental Status of seawater, according to the MSFD 56/2008. On the Adriatic coast, the Western Adriatic Coastal Current (WACC) transports these riverine inputs predominantly southward.

The Hypothesis: To evaluate the impact of the Chienti River, we tested the hypothesis (H1) that river runoff affects environmental quality and fauna, with stronger, asymmetrical impacts downstream due to coastal currents, against the null hypothesis (H0) of no significant impact and no current-driven pattern.

Methodology

Physical-Chemical Parameters

CTD Profiling: Measurement of Salinity, Temperature, and Depth along the water column. Turbidity: Estimation of water transparency using a Secchi Disk. TRIX Index: Calculation of the trophic state combining nutrients and Chlorophyll-a data.

Deployment of the CTD Profiler and Secchi Disk.

Biological Indicators: Water Column

Phytopigments: Analyzed Chlorophyll-a as an indicator of phytoplankton biomass. Zooplankton: Assessed community abundance, applying the Shannon-Wiener Index and Pielou’s Evenness Index to evaluate species diversity.

Deploying the Plankton Net for zooplankton sampling.

Biological Indicators: Benthic Fauna

Meiofauna & Macrofauna: Quantified benthic organisms (e.g., nematodes, copepods, polychaetes), conducting community analyses based on abundance, taxa richness, and biodiversity indices to identify pollution and organic enrichment.

Benthic sampling techniques and sediment processing on deck.

Data Integration & Statistics

Processed water and sediment samples for Total Suspended Matter (TSM), Inorganic Matter (IM), and Organic Matter (OM/TOM). Evaluated environmental conditions using the Nematodes/Copepods ratio, supported by Tukey post-hoc statistical testing.

Key Takeaways

  • Tracing the Organic Load

    Biogeochemical analyses successfully traced the Chienti river discharge. The Downstream transect (T4) showed significantly higher Total Organic Matter (TOM) and TRIX values (5.52, exceeding the good/poor threshold) compared to the Upstream and Estuary stations, confirming the southward push of the WACC.

    TRIX values across the transects.

  • Benthic Ecological Disequilibrium

    The benthic biological data revealed an altered condition across the entire study area. Taxa richness was low (4-8 taxa), and the Nematode/Copepod (N/C) ratio peaked dramatically at the Downstream transect (N/C = 196.0). Given the greater tolerance of nematodes to high organic loads and consequent reduced oxygen, in contrast to highly sensitive copepods, this big dominance serves as a bio-indicator of organic enrichment.

    Taxa distribution and abundance of Meiofauna demonstrating variability across transects.

  • Zooplankton Response

    Conversely, the Shannon-Wiener index indicated higher zooplankton diversity at the estuary (1.367) and downstream (1.425) stations compared to upstream. This suggests that the river's nutrient enrichment stimulated phytoplankton growth in the water column, which in turn provided an abundant food source that supported a more diverse zooplankton community.

    Shannon-Wiener index of species diversity demonstrating variability across transects.
